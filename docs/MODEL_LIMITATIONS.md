# Model Limitations

This model is a planning-support prototype for comparing public EV charging candidate sites. It does **not** provide official engineering, permitting, utility, procurement, Fire Marshal/AHJ, ADA, stormwater, or construction approval.

Use the model to screen sites, compare tradeoffs, identify questions, and support discussion. Do not use it as final design, final approval, construction guidance, or procurement direction.

## Demand and utilization limitations

The model estimates potential charging demand using planning proxies such as nearby households, jobs, traffic exposure, amenities, local EV adoption assumptions, site type, dwell time, and site-specific manual inputs.

These variables are useful for early screening, but they do not guarantee real charger utilization. Actual use can change because of pricing, network reliability, parking rules, charger uptime, nearby competing chargers, local travel behavior, seasonal demand, future EV adoption, and policy changes.

## Electrical and construction limitations

Electrical feasibility is treated as a screening estimate, not a utility-approved design decision.

The model can flag higher-risk situations such as possible service-upgrade need, longer trenching or wire runs, and limited planning confidence. Final feasibility still requires field verification, utility coordination, electrical engineering review, interconnection review, and site-specific cost estimates.

## Safety, ADA, fire access, and stormwater limitations

The review flags are meant to identify possible site-review questions. They are not official AHJ determinations and do not replace code review, Fire Marshal review, ADA design review, stormwater review, or permitting.

A flagged site is not automatically a bad site. It means the site may need more review before being advanced, costed, designed, or presented as a near-term implementation candidate.

## Right-sizing limitations

Port recommendations are planning estimates based on assumptions about sessions, dwell time, utilization targets, future growth, and scenario stress tests.

Final port counts should be checked against budget, utility constraints, charger type, operating policy, public-access rules, stakeholder goals, ADA layout, maintenance capacity, and expected demand growth.

## Data limitations

Some data comes from public datasets, some comes from reviewed source extracts, and some comes from assumptions or manual review. Public datasets can be incomplete, delayed, spatially coarse, inconsistent across jurisdictions, or unable to show site-level conditions.

Data confidence should be reviewed along with the readiness score. A high-scoring site with weak data should not be treated the same as a high-scoring site with stronger field-verified information.

## Appropriate use

Appropriate uses include:

- comparing candidate sites at a planning level;
- identifying likely strengths and constraints;
- prioritizing follow-up data collection;
- explaining why one site may be more ready than another;
- supporting stakeholder discussion;
- preparing questions for utility, engineering, ADA, Fire Marshal/AHJ, stormwater, or site-owner review.

Inappropriate uses include:

- final engineering design;
- permitting approval;
- utility approval;
- final procurement decisions;
- construction authorization;
- claims that a site is officially compliant or ready to build.
